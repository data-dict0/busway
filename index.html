Every day, an average of 183,000 people ride the buses plying the dedicated bus lanes along EDSA.
That number is often cited in debates regarding the program's funding. But its scale often gets lost in the conversation. Since 2021, the EDSA busway has become an integral part of public transportation in Metro Manila where horrendous traffic is a daily struggle.
Despite this direct benefit to commuters, transport advocates have consistently faced problems securing more funding for the project.
For next year, they almost had none until a public backlash pushed legislators to allocate P75 million for the project.
Advocates say that funding is still not enough.

Why the EDSA busway matters to commuters

Oct. 26, 2026

And that number is just the daily average. Robert Siy, a transport economist and part of the Move As One Coalition, a commuter group pushing for more funding for the EDSA busway, said the busway can be expanded to cover as many as 2.5 million passengers everyday.
To put that into context, busway ridership peaked at about 400,000 a day some time in late 2022, according to data from the Transportation department. For all of 2025, busway ridership grew nearly 6% year-on-year to 66.7 million. It was down about a tenth from previous year for the first six months of 2026.
Funding was not the first hurdle the EDSA busway has faced. In February 2025, the Metropolitan Manila Development Authority (MMDA), which coordinates traffic regulations in Metro Manila, floated the idea of phasing out the busway to remove its dedicated EDSA lane and open them up to other vehicles.
At the time, MMDA Chairman Romando Artes said the Metro Rail Transit -3 (MRT-3), which runs parallel with the busway in most stations, could instead accommodate busway passengers after its rehabilitation finished. Repairs have completed, but the phase-out plan has since been dropped.
Siy reiterated that "redundancy" in public transport, which in this case means having both the EDSA busway and the MRT-3, is good and benefits passengers, especially with "occasional breakdowns of the MRT-3."
"EDSA busway adds to the public transport capacity on EDSA. Without the EDSA busway, queues and waiting would be much, much longer," he said.
The 28-kilometer busway has 26 stations running from Monumento in Caloocan in the north to the Paranaque Integrated Terminal Exchange in the south and back. The southbound route has one more station than the northbound route while some stations are only served in a single direction.
On weekdays, Monumento in Caloocan and Roosevelt in Quezon City saw the largest number of passengers between Jan. 2025 and June 2026, government data showed. Interestingly, these stations also house the terminal stations for another rail system, the Light Rail Transit-1 (LRT-1).

According to the EDSA busway website, its route connects to 10 of the 13 MRT-3 stations. This means passengers are in close proximity to those rail stations when disembarking from the busway, and vice-versa. Such convenience, Siy said, allows passengers to easily navigate between these modes of transport when commuting.
But in most cases, a Data Dictionary analysis showed no clear relationship between taking the busway and the MRT-3 during weekdays. In short, taking one mode of transportation does not determine riding the other, or not riding either of them. Such relationship can only be seen, albeit very weak, in terminal stations like Taft Avenue.
For Siy, this shows that demand for reliable public transportation continues to outstrip supply and that both the busway and the MRT-3 co-exist to try to meet that demand.

"Some want to economize and go for the cheapest fare, so they prefer MRT-3, no matter what the inconvenience or discomfort. The Busway costs more and usually requires a much longer walk, but some are willing to endure these disadvantages because they are rewarded with a seated, more comfortable ride," Siy explained.
"So the demand could be split between these two groups. MRT-3 and Busway serve different purposes for different groups," he added.
Incentives matter, too. From late 2021 to 2023, when the government offered free busway rides, busway ridership exceeded that of the MRT-3. Almost immediately after free rides were discontinued, busway ridership fell and has continued to hover at about 40% of MRT-3 ridership, data showed.
At the same time, the national government started offering discounted MRT-3 rides last March to cushion the impact of surging oil prices on commuter fares.
